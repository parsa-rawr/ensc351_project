Hardware Validation & Diagnostic System

Starter design pack for the BeagleY-AI: a 12-bit SPI ADC, two test circuits, and the CircuitCheck dashboard prototype. Its readings are simulated; it does not connect to the board.

01 · Shared connections

BeagleY-AI ↔ MCP3208

Use the board's 3.3 V rail for the ADC supply and reference. Keep every ground connected together. The MCP3208 reads the divider on CH0 or the RC node on CH2 and sends the digital reading over SPI.

Important: BeagleY-AI GPIO is 3.3 V tolerant. Do not put 5 V on GPIO or ADC inputs. Set the MCP3208 VREF to 3.3 V so the measured inputs stay in the 0–3.3 V range.

02 · First test circuit

Resistor-divider voltage check

This is the easiest circuit to bring up. Two equal resistors divide 3.3 V roughly in half. The ADC should read about 1.65 V on CH0.

Expected result: Vnode = 3.3 × 10k / (10k + 10k) = 1.65 V. Set a reasonable tolerance in software, for example ±0.15 V, then report PASS or FAIL.

Build it on the breadboard

  1. Connect BeagleY-AI 3V3 to one end of R1.
  2. Connect R1's other end to a shared row: this is the test node.
  3. Connect the node to R2 and MCP3208 CH0.
  4. Connect the other end of R2 to ground.
  5. Join BeagleY-AI ground and both MCP3208 grounds.

What to capture as proof

Take one photo showing the board, ADC, and divider on the breadboard. Capture your program printing the measured voltage and PASS result. Change the divider or disconnect a lead and capture a FAIL result.

Good first milestone
03 · Timed measurement

RC charge-time test

GPIO22 (physical HAT pin 15) drives five 10 kΩ resistors in series (50 kΩ total). The MCP3208 samples the 10 µF capacitor node on CH2 (pin 3). Expected time constant: τ = RC = 0.50 s.

Careful with electrolytic capacitors: connect the positive lead to the RC node and the negative lead to ground. Confirm the capacitor voltage rating is above 3.3 V. Keep the ADC input between 0 V and 3.3 V.

Test sequence

  1. Drive GPIO22 LOW and sample the initial discharge phase.
  2. Drive GPIO22 HIGH and measure charging on CH2; estimate τ at 63.2% of the voltage rise.
  3. Drive GPIO22 LOW and measure discharging; estimate τ at 36.8% of the charged voltage.
  4. Compare both estimates with 0.50 s and save the CSV samples.

Run the matching repo test

./build/app/board_diagnostics --test rc --resistance 50000 --capacitance-uf 10 --tau-tolerance 0.25 --interval-ms 5 --rc-channel 2 --drive GPIO22 --csv hardware-rc-001.csv

This command matches the current project configuration. It requires the BeagleY-AI wiring and SPI setup described in the repository README.

What this proves

It demonstrates a timed test with real hardware, repeated ADC measurements, and a calculated result. Include the voltage-versus-time plot and a terminal line showing measured τ compared with the expected value.

How to turn this into your real UI

Connect the buttons to your test program through a local web server or a simple desktop UI. Replace the simulated readings with the voltage values read from the MCP3208. Take a screenshot while the real circuit test is running for your project proof.

Design assumptions: BeagleY-AI 40-pin header; MCP3208 powered at 3.3 V; 10 kΩ divider; 50 kΩ + 10 µF RC circuit (expected τ ≈ 0.5 s). Verify SPI enablement and pin multiplexing on the project image before using the wiring.
